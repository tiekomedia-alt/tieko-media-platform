## 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual and interaction system for the Tieko Media platform.

It converts Tieko’s established creative direction into reusable rules for:

- Brand expression
- Colour
- Typography
- Layout
- Spacing
- Photography
- Illustration
- Texture
- Icons
- Components
- Responsive behaviour
- Motion
- Accessibility
- The Tieko Àmì Ribbon System
- Stitch design generation
- Codex implementation

The system must preserve a recognisable identity across pages without forcing every page to use the same composition.

## 2. Brand character

The Tieko Media platform should feel:

- African
- Intelligent
- Editorial
- Cultured
- Precise
- Tactile
- Contemporary
- Premium
- Useful
- Confident
- Human
- Technically credible

The visual identity must not feel:

- Like a generic technology startup
- Like a conventional law-firm template
- Like a social-media marketing template
- Excessively corporate
- Artificially futuristic
- Neon
- Glossy without substance
- Overcrowded
- Decorative without purpose
- Dependent on stereotypes of Africa

## 3. Strategic visual idea

Tieko Media sits at the intersection of:

- African creative intelligence
- Digital infrastructure
- Research
- Business building
- Visibility
- Compliance
- Products

The website should therefore combine two visual worlds.

### 3.1 Editorial and cultural world

Expressed through:

- Warm paper-like surfaces
- Serif typography
- African photography
- Tactile texture
- Sculptural red forms
- Restrained gold details
- Print-registration references
- Generous composition

### 3.2 Digital and infrastructural world

Expressed through:

- Precise grids
- Technical lines
- Clear information hierarchy
- Data visualisation
- Structured cards
- Functional interfaces
- Responsive systems
- Measurable interaction
- Fast performance

The design must not choose between these worlds. Its distinctiveness comes from combining them.

## 4. Core design principles

### 4.1 Culture through structure

African identity should be expressed through visual rhythm, materials, people, symbols and context. It should not depend on generic masks, maps or repeated decorative patterns.

### 4.2 Editorial clarity

Pages should feel composed, not assembled from interchangeable software cards.

### 4.3 Red with purpose

Red directs attention, creates movement and carries the brand. It must not colour every surface or compete with every message.

### 4.4 Texture with restraint

Texture should make the interface feel tactile without reducing legibility or increasing page weight unnecessarily.

### 4.5 Evidence is visual content

Products, reports, diagrams, client work and measurable outcomes should occupy prominent visual space.

### 4.6 Responsive, not reduced

Mobile must be intentionally composed. It must not be a compressed desktop design.

### 4.7 Motion explains

Animation should reveal relationships, reinforce direction or provide feedback. It should not exist merely to impress.

### 4.8 Accessibility is part of quality

Contrast, focus, reduced motion, readable type and usable controls are core components of the visual system.

## 5. Logo system

The final production logo assets must be approved separately.

Required logo variants:

- Primary full-colour logo
- Dark logo for light backgrounds
- Light logo for dark or red backgrounds
- Single-colour black logo
- Single-colour white logo
- Compact mark
- Favicon and application icon
- Social profile mark

### Logo rules

- Maintain a defined clear space around the logo.
- Do not stretch or distort the logo.
- Do not recolour it outside approved variants.
- Do not place it over visually noisy areas without sufficient separation.
- Do not combine it with the Àmì Ribbon in a way that makes the ribbon appear to be part of the logo unless an approved lock-up is created.
- Avoid placing the registration number beneath the logo in primary navigation.
- Use the company’s verified legal identity in the footer or relevant trust area.

Exact clear-space and minimum-size measurements will be added when vector logo assets are approved.

### 6.2 Colour usage

Recommended visual distribution on light editorial pages:

- 60 to 70 percent warm ivory, soft paper or white
- 20 to 30 percent ink, charcoal, imagery and typography
- 5 to 10 percent Tieko red
- Less than 5 percent restrained gold

This is a compositional guide, not a rigid calculation.

### 6.3 Red usage

Tieko Red may be used for:

- Large Àmì Ribbon surfaces
- Large graphic forms
- Selected section backgrounds
- Important visual markers
- Large display accents
- Decorative data highlights

Action Red should be used for:

- Primary buttons on light backgrounds
- Small red links
- Focused interface actions
- Form-error details
- Text that must meet contrast requirements

Pure white may be used on sufficiently dark red surfaces after contrast validation.

Tieko Red must not be used for small body text unless the exact colour pairing passes the required contrast threshold.

### 6.4 Gold usage

Gold is a supporting precision colour, not a luxury effect.

Use it for:

- Fine rules
- Grid markers
- Diagram nodes
- Section indices
- Small registration-style symbols
- Restrained data accents

Do not use:

- Gold gradients
- Metallic glow
- Large gold backgrounds
- Gold body text on ivory
- Decorative gold borders around every component

### 6.5 Dark sections

Dark sections should use Ink Black or Charcoal rather than blue-black or generic navy. This preserves the print-editorial character.

Red remains the principal accent. White and warm grey provide text contrast.

### 6.6 Contrast requirement

All colour combinations used for text and interactive controls must be tested against WCAG 2.2 Level AA.

Decorative brand colours do not override accessibility requirements.

## 7. Typography

The typography system uses three roles.

### 7.1 Display condensed

**Recommended family:** Barlow Condensed  
**Fallbacks:** Arial Narrow, sans-serif

Purpose:

- Homepage statements
- Campaign-like headings
- Service-page hero headings
- Section titles requiring force and economy
- Selected numerals

Characteristics:

- Tall
- Direct
- High-impact
- Space-efficient
- Similar in spirit to the established carousel direction

Rules:

- Use bold or extra-bold weights.
- Avoid long paragraphs.
- Maintain deliberate line breaks.
- Avoid excessive letter spacing in large headings.
- Do not use all caps for every heading.
- Test narrow letterforms on small screens.

### 7.2 Editorial serif

**Recommended family:** Source Serif 4  
**Fallbacks:** Georgia, serif

Purpose:

- Selected thought-leadership statements
- Report titles
- Pull quotations
- Editorial introductions
- Display-level explanatory text

Rules:

- Use selectively.
- Preserve readable line length.
- Avoid very light weights on textured backgrounds.
- Do not use italic serif as decoration throughout the interface.

### 7.3 Interface sans serif

**Recommended family:** Inter  
**Fallbacks:** system-ui, Arial, sans-serif

Purpose:

- Body copy
- Navigation
- Buttons
- Forms
- Captions
- Metadata
- Tables
- Data labels
- Interface feedback

Rules:

- Prioritise readability.
- Use sentence case for most labels.
- Avoid excessive uppercase body copy.
- Use tabular numerals where data comparison benefits.

### 7.4 Font delivery

Production fonts should:

- Have appropriate licences
- Be self-hosted where practical
- Use WOFF2
- Load only required families, weights and subsets
- Use font-display behaviour that protects readability
- Provide metrics-compatible fallbacks where possible

### 7.5 Type scale

Suggested fluid type roles:

| Role | Small viewport | Large viewport | Typical font |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Display XL | 3.25rem | 7rem | Barlow Condensed |
| Display L | 2.75rem | 5rem | Barlow Condensed |
| Heading 1 | 2.5rem | 4.5rem | Barlow Condensed |
| Heading 2 | 2rem | 3.5rem | Barlow Condensed or Source Serif 4 |
| Heading 3 | 1.5rem | 2.25rem | Barlow Condensed or Source Serif 4 |
| Heading 4 | 1.25rem | 1.5rem | Inter |
| Lead | 1.125rem | 1.375rem | Inter or Source Serif 4 |
| Body | 1rem | 1.125rem | Inter |
| Small | 0.875rem | 0.9375rem | Inter |
| Label | 0.75rem | 0.8125rem | Inter |

Fluid sizing should use controlled clamp expressions rather than abrupt jumps.

### 7.6 Reading rules

- Body line height: approximately 1.55 to 1.75
- Editorial lead line height: approximately 1.35 to 1.55
- Display line height: approximately 0.88 to 1.02
- Body line length: approximately 55 to 75 characters
- Avoid justified text
- Avoid centre-aligned paragraphs longer than three short lines
- Avoid orphaned single words in major display headings where reasonable
- Public copy should use professional British English
- Public copy should not use em dashes

## 8. Grid and layout

### 8.1 Layout philosophy

The grid should feel editorial and precise, with intentional asymmetry.

Layouts may combine:

- Large text fields
- Narrow metadata columns
- Cropped visual forms
- Offset imagery
- Full-bleed ribbon elements
- Quiet technical grids
- Strong margins

Asymmetry must remain balanced and readable.

### 8.2 Content widths

Suggested maximum widths:

| Content type | Maximum width |
|---|---:|
| Wide composition | 1440px |
| Standard page container | 1280px |
| Editorial content | 760px |
| Form content | 680px |
| Narrow legal copy | 720px |

### 8.3 Grid columns

| Viewport | Grid |
|---|---|
| Small mobile | 4 columns |
| Large mobile | 4 columns |
| Tablet | 8 columns |
| Laptop and desktop | 12 columns |
| Wide screen | 12 columns with controlled maximum width |

### 8.4 Gutters and margins

Suggested starting values:

- Small mobile outer margin: 20px
- Large mobile outer margin: 24px
- Tablet outer margin: 32px
- Laptop outer margin: 48px
- Desktop outer margin: 64px
- Wide screen: centred container with generous breathing room

Values may adapt fluidly.

### 8.5 Editorial grid lines

Faint grid lines may appear in:

- Hero backgrounds
- Report and intelligence sections
- Data-led diagrams
- Section transitions
- Selected empty areas

Grid lines must:

- Remain low contrast
- Avoid crossing body text aggressively
- Be implemented efficiently
- Disappear or simplify on small screens
- Be decorative to assistive technology

## 10. Shape and border language

### 10.1 Corners

The brand should not rely on excessively rounded software-style cards.

Recommended corner behaviour:

- Buttons: modest radius from 4px to 8px
- Form controls: 4px to 8px
- Editorial cards: 0px to 8px
- Images: square, lightly rounded or custom-cropped depending on composition
- Pills: limited to tags, statuses and compact controls

Avoid making every element a pill or floating rounded rectangle.

### 10.2 Borders

Borders should be:

- Fine
- Warm grey or low-opacity black
- Used to organise information
- Combined with spacing rather than heavy shadows

### 10.3 Shadows

Use shadows sparingly.

Appropriate uses:

- Floating ribbon photography
- Menus
- Dialogs
- Paper-like report previews
- Selected editorial layers

Avoid:

- Strong card shadows everywhere
- Coloured glow
- Neon shadow
- Overly soft SaaS-style floating surfaces

## 11. Tieko Àmì Ribbon System

## 11.1 Definition

The Tieko Àmì Ribbon System is the primary expressive visual device of the platform.

It translates the red curling forms established in Tieko’s social creative direction into a responsive digital language.

The system represents:

- Movement
- Direction
- Connection
- African identity
- Intelligence flowing through infrastructure
- Continuity between ideas and execution
- Visibility
- Growth

### 11.2 Expression A: Sculptural Ribbon

A dimensional red form that:

- Curls
- Folds
- Twists
- Overlaps
- Enters from page edges
- Frames people or content
- Creates controlled depth

It should feel closer to folded textile, paper or sculptural material than plastic or liquid.

Surface characteristics may include:

- Fine textile grain
- Paper fibre
- Subtle embossed African geometry
- Soft natural shadow
- Tonal red variation

It must not use:

- Neon glow
- Chrome
- Glassmorphism
- Wet plastic
- Excessive 3D gloss
- Random looping without compositional purpose

### 11.3 Expression B: Graphic Àmì Path

A flatter red path containing restrained symbolic apertures, marks or cut-outs.

It may:

- Travel behind imagery
- Connect process steps
- Create a directional line
- Frame a service group
- Carry section numbering
- Form a background rhythm
- Act as a mask

The graphic path should not imitate a specific sacred or culturally restricted symbol without research and approval.

### 11.4 Expression C: Woven Ribbon

A textured textile expression may appear in selected cultural, content or campaign contexts.

It should be:

- Based on authentic visual research
- Used respectfully
- Subordinate to content
- Optimised for performance
- Avoided as a generic pan-African decoration

### 11.5 Placement rules

Recommended placements:

- One major hero expression
- Selected section transitions
- One or two supporting moments on a long page
- Final conversion section
- Report or case-study covers
- Image masks
- Empty-space anchors

Do not place a large ribbon in every section.

### 11.6 Directional function

The ribbon should guide attention towards:

- Key statement
- Featured product
- Important proof
- Process progression
- Book a Call
- Commission a Report

Its direction must not lead the eye away from the primary action.

### 11.7 Responsive behaviour

#### Desktop

- May enter from multiple edges
- May use overlapping depth
- May occupy substantial negative space
- May connect distant content zones
- May move subtly with scroll

#### Tablet

- Reduce depth and overlap
- Preserve clear content boundaries
- Simplify long paths
- Avoid covering interactive regions

#### Mobile

- Use cropped edge fragments
- Use flatter versions
- Avoid complex multi-loop compositions
- Keep important content within safe zones
- Never create horizontal overflow
- Reduce texture resolution and motion
- Permit complete omission where composition is stronger without it

### 11.8 Motion behaviour

Permitted motion:

- Slow positional drift
- Subtle reveal
- Gentle fold transition
- Path progression
- Small parallax within strict limits
- Directional movement towards a CTA

Motion must:

- Remain subtle
- Avoid delaying content
- Avoid scroll hijacking
- Avoid continuous distracting loops
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion
- Preserve meaning without animation

### 11.9 Implementation hierarchy

Preferred implementation order:

1. CSS or SVG for flat graphic paths
2. Optimised SVG masks for structured forms
3. AVIF or WebP for highly textured sculptural assets
4. Video only when essential and performance-approved
5. Canvas or WebGL only if a clear benefit justifies cost and accessibility complexity

### 11.10 Accessibility

Ribbon assets are normally decorative and should be hidden from assistive technology.

If a ribbon contains information, that information must also exist as accessible text or semantic structure.

## 12. Texture system

### 12.1 Paper texture

A quiet paper texture may be used on major ivory surfaces.

Requirements:

- Low contrast
- Seamless or sufficiently large
- Optimised
- No interference with text
- No visible tiling
- Disabled where it harms performance

### 12.2 Print grain

Fine print grain may appear in:

- Hero imagery
- Report covers
- Selected case-study visuals
- Ribbon assets

It should not appear over interface controls or small type.

### 12.3 Technical overlay

Fine grid and registration overlays may support the digital-infrastructure theme.

They must remain decorative, low contrast and responsive.

## 13. Photography

### 13.1 Photography direction

Photography should prioritise:

- African founders
- Builders
- Researchers
- Lawyers
- Creatives
- Teams
- Working environments
- Real technology use
- Business decision-making
- Production processes
- Lagos and African urban context
- Products in use

### 13.2 Image qualities

Images should feel:

- Observational
- Intelligent
- Warm
- Composed
- Authentic
- Textural
- Editorial

Avoid:

- Generic handshake photography
- Artificially diverse boardroom stock images
- Excessive posing
- Futuristic holograms
- Generic glowing screens
- Stereotypical poverty imagery
- Unrelated African symbols
- Images that present Africa as one undifferentiated place

### 13.3 Composition

Photography may be:

- Cropped by the ribbon
- Partly overlaid by technical grids
- Placed asymmetrically
- Paired with large type
- Presented as editorial spreads
- Layered with paper-like captions

Faces must not be obscured unintentionally. Important subjects must remain within responsive safe zones.

### 13.4 Image rights

Every image must have:

- Confirmed ownership or licence
- Attribution where required
- Consent where identifiable people are featured
- Alt-text decision
- Source record

## 14. Illustration and diagrams

Illustration should be used to explain:

- Processes
- Systems
- Research methods
- Digital presence
- Media flows
- Data relationships
- Product architecture
- Business journeys

Visual style:

- Black line work
- Tieko red shapes
- Restrained gold nodes
- Ivory background
- Editorial simplicity
- Clear labels
- Minimal visual noise

Precise diagrams should be produced as SVG or code-based graphics, not AI-generated raster images.

## 15. Data visualisation

Data visualisations should use:

- Ink Black
- Tieko Red
- Action Red
- Restrained Gold
- Warm neutral shades

Requirements:

- Do not communicate through colour alone.
- Provide labels or legends.
- Support accessible summaries.
- Use appropriate chart types.
- Avoid decorative 3D charts.
- Maintain readable text on mobile.
- State sources and dates.
- Distinguish estimates from observed data.
- Avoid fabricated data in mock-ups that could be mistaken for results.

## 16. Iconography

Icons should be:

- Simple
- Geometric
- Consistent in stroke weight
- Legible at small sizes
- Secondary to text
- Culturally neutral unless intentionally researched

Use icons for:

- Supporting labels
- Actions
- Process steps
- Service cues
- Form feedback

Do not use an icon alone when its meaning is ambiguous.

Avoid mixing multiple unrelated icon libraries.

## 17. Buttons and links

### 17.1 Primary button

Use for:

- Book a Call
- Commission a Report where contextually primary
- Critical form submission

Recommended treatment:

- Action Red background
- White text
- Modest radius
- Clear hover, focus, active and disabled states
- No gradient
- No glow

### 17.2 Secondary button

Recommended treatments:

- Ink outline on light background
- White outline on dark background
- Quiet ivory surface where appropriate

### 17.3 Text link

Text links must:

- Be visually recognisable
- Use underline or another non-colour-only cue where needed
- Have visible hover and focus states
- Describe the destination

### 17.4 CTA hierarchy

One visual region should not contain multiple equally dominant CTAs.

Recommended hierarchy:

1. Book a Call
2. Contextual secondary action
3. Supporting text links

## 18. Cards

Card types may include:

- Service card
- Case-study card
- Product card
- Insight card
- Report card
- Team card
- Proof card

Cards must not all share the same layout.

### Service cards

Should communicate:

- Problem or outcome
- Service name
- Short explanation
- Clear destination

### Report cards

Should communicate:

- Report type
- Title
- Publication date
- Geographic or sector scope
- Paid, sample or public status

### Insight cards

Should communicate:

- Topic
- Title
- Summary
- Author or date where useful
- Reading time where useful

### Card interaction

- Entire-card links must remain accessible.
- Hover cannot be the only state indicating interactivity.
- Cards must work without motion.
- Focus order must remain logical.
- Avoid nested conflicting links.

## 19. Forms

Form styling must feel serious, clear and trustworthy.

Requirements:

- Visible labels
- Helpful instructions
- Large touch targets
- Strong focus states
- Clear required and optional indicators
- Field-level errors
- Form-level error summary for longer forms
- Success states that do not rely on colour alone
- No placeholder used as the only label
- No unnecessary fields
- No deceptive consent patterns

Recommended visual treatment:

- Light paper or white surface
- Ink text
- Warm grey border
- Action Red focus or error treatment
- Green confirmation treatment with text and icon
- Clear spacing between fields

## 20. Navigation design

### Desktop

- Compact, confident header
- Clear wordmark
- Grouped Services menu
- Book a Call as the primary button
- No registration number beneath the logo
- No excessive transparency that harms contrast

### Mobile

- Clearly labelled menu button
- Full-height or large panel with controlled hierarchy
- Book a Call near the top
- Expandable service groups
- Visible close control
- Body-scroll lock
- Accessible focus management

### Sticky behaviour

The header may become sticky after initial scroll if:

- It remains compact
- It does not cover content
- It does not create distracting animation
- Anchor destinations account for its height

## 21. Hero design

The homepage hero must:

- State what Tieko is
- Establish African editorial character
- Contain Book a Call
- Provide one secondary action
- Use the Àmì Ribbon as a meaningful framing device
- Remain clear on mobile
- Avoid generic cosmic or futuristic imagery
- Avoid autoplay video unless separately approved
- Avoid placing essential copy inside raster images

Potential composition:

- Large condensed statement
- Short sans-serif supporting copy
- CTA pair
- Sculptural ribbon entering from one edge
- Authentic African subject or product evidence
- Faint technical grid
- Restrained gold markers

## 22. Section design

A long page should alternate intentionally between:

- Editorial ivory sections
- White evidence sections
- Dark high-contrast sections
- Red conversion moments
- Image-led spreads
- Data or diagram sections

Do not alternate background colour mechanically. Changes must support content hierarchy.

Reports should feel rigorous and valuable.

Recommended elements:

- Strong report cover
- Edition or date
- Geographic scope
- Methodology label
- Author and reviewer
- Executive-summary treatment
- Sample-page preview
- Restricted-content indication
- Commission a Report CTA
- Source and limitation presentation

Report covers may use:

- Àmì Ribbon
- Data grids
- Large condensed titles
- Editorial serif details

## 24. Case-study design

Case studies should be structured as evidence.

Recommended visual sequence:

1. Outcome-led title
2. Project metadata
3. Challenge
4. Diagnosis or research
5. Strategy
6. Execution
7. Deliverables
8. Results
9. Client evidence
10. Related service
11. Book a Call

Use measured outcomes visually where verified.

## 25. Article design

Articles must prioritise reading.

Requirements:

- Clear title and summary
- Author and date
- Updated date where relevant
- Comfortable line length
- Table of contents for long pieces
- Strong heading hierarchy
- Accessible figures and captions
- Source section where required
- Related service and content
- Restrained CTA placement
- No intrusive pop-ups

The decorative system should become quieter inside the main reading column.

## 26. Motion system

### Permitted motion categories

- Page or section reveal
- Menu transition
- Accordion expansion
- Button feedback
- Form-state feedback
- Ribbon drift or progression
- Data transition
- Image-mask reveal

### Timing guidance

- Interface feedback: 100 to 200 milliseconds
- Standard transitions: 180 to 300 milliseconds
- Editorial reveals: 300 to 600 milliseconds
- Ambient ribbon motion: slow and subtle

### Easing

Use natural easing. Avoid elastic bounce unless a specific product interaction justifies it.

### Reduced motion

When reduced motion is requested:

- Remove parallax
- Remove ambient looping
- Replace movement with opacity or instant state changes
- Preserve all information and controls

- Secondary CTA
- Desktop, tablet and mobile requirements
- Colour roles
- Typography roles
- Àmì Ribbon instructions
- Photography direction
- Grid and spacing direction
- Accessibility expectations
- Performance awareness
- Explicit exclusions

Every prompt must explicitly exclude:

- Generic SaaS gradients
- Neon
- Excessive glassmorphism

- Crowded layouts
- Stock handshake imagery

## 31. Design review checklist

A page is ready for engineering review when:

1. Its purpose is clear within seconds.
2. The primary action is obvious.
3. The layout works on mobile, tablet and desktop.
4. The ribbon improves the composition.
5. The page remains strong without motion.
6. Text contrast passes.
7. Focus states are designed.
8. Content is not hidden inside images.
9. The visual hierarchy matches user intent.
10. The page uses authentic and licensed imagery.
11. Performance-heavy elements are justified.
12. Error, loading and empty states are included where relevant.
13. Components align with existing system rules.
14. The page does not look like a generic template.
15. Professional or regulated-service boundaries are represented correctly.
